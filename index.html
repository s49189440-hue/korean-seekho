👑 King Sejong the Great

The King Behind Hangul

King Sejong the Great was the fourth king of the Joseon Dynasty in Korea. He ruled from 1418 to 1450.

During his time, many ordinary people found it difficult to read and write because Classical Chinese characters were widely used in education and official writing.

King Sejong wanted ordinary people to have a writing system that was easier to learn. He supported the creation of a new Korean alphabet called Hangul.

Hangul was created in 1443 and officially introduced in 1446 through a publication called Hunminjeongeum.

King Sejong also supported education, science, agriculture and cultural development. Today, he is remembered as one of Korea's most respected historical leaders.

Fun Fact: Hunminjeongeum means "The Correct Sounds for the Instruction of the People."

📜 History of Hangul

The Beginning of a New Writing System

Before 1443: Classical Chinese characters were widely used in official writing and education.

1443: Hangul was created under King Sejong's leadership.

1446: Hunminjeongeum was published to explain the new writing system.

October 9: South Korea celebrates Hangul Day every year.

Hangul has a logical structure. Its letters are combined into syllable blocks, making it an interesting writing system to learn.
